      How scoring works

      Ratings use the Elo system, the same idea chess uses. Everyone starts at 1000. When you win you take points from the other side, and how many depends on how surprising the result was.

      One match

      1. Work out the winner's expected chance of winning from the rating gap:
        expected = 1 / (1 + 10(loser − winner) / 400)
      2. Points moved = 32 × (1 − expected), rounded. 32 is the K-factor: the most one match can move.
      3. The winner gains those points and the loser loses the same amount.
      WinnerLoserExpectedPoints

      1200 (big favourite)100076%±8

      2v2

      Each team's rating is the average of its two players. The points are worked out the same way, then every winner gains them and every loser loses them.

      The three ladders

      Overall counts every match, 1v1 and 2v2. The 1v1 and 2v2 tabs keep separate ratings that only count that format, so you can see who is strongest at each.

      Good to know

      • The score (e.g. 11–4) is saved for the record but doesn't change points. Only the win counts.
      • (P) marks players with fewer than 5 games, whose rating is still settling.
      • Ratings are recalculated from the full match history every time, in the order matches were played. Deleting a wrong match corrects everyone's numbers.
      • Hiding a player removes them from the ladder but keeps their matches, so nobody else's rating changes.
